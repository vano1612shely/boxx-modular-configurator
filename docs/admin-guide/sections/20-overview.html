Chapter 1The admin at a glance

Read this chapter once. It explains what the admin panel controls, how its sections relate to one another, and the handful of habits that apply to every screen in it. Everything after this chapter assumes you know it.

What this system does

The product has two halves that share one database.

  • The configurator, at /configurator, is what your customers use. They answer two short questions, are shown the building that fits, walk through it in 3D, furnish the rooms, choose entrances, and send you a request for a quote.
  • The admin, at /admin, is where all of that comes from. Product ranges, building sizes, room layouts, furniture, 3D models, photographs — even the wording of the opening questions — are records you edit here.

Nothing the customer sees is fixed in the software. If it appears on screen, there is a record behind it, and you can change it.

Signing in

Address /admin
  1. Open /admin in your browser. If you are not signed in, the login screen appears.
  2. Enter the email address and password of your administrator account.
  3. Select Login. You land on the dashboard.

Your session stays active in that browser until you sign out from the account menu, so you will not normally see this screen again day to day.

The screen

Every page in the admin has the same three parts.

The navigation is grouped into four sections. The order is deliberate: it runs from what you set up once, through what you edit often, to what the system produces for you.

How the catalogue fits together

Catalogue records point at one another in a chain. This matters in practice, because you cannot point at something that does not exist yet — the chain is also the order in which you build a new product range.

Region Where you sell
Product Line A range, e.g. BOXXPlex
Building Model One size of that range
Rooms Drawn in the Scene Editor, typed by Room Type
Exterior Options Doors, stairs, ramps at each entrance
Furniture Packages Grouped by Furniture Tier, matched to Room Types

A customer's journey runs along the same chain in the opposite direction: they arrive in a region, pick a line, are given the model that fits, then furnish its rooms and choose its entrances.

The pattern every collection follows

Regions, Product Lines, Building Models and the rest all behave identically. Learn it once and it applies everywhere.

Opening a collection

Select its name in the navigation. You get the list view — every record in that collection, one per row. Its address follows a fixed pattern, so you can bookmark or share it: /admin/collections/<collection>.

Creating a record

  1. Select Create New at the top right of the list view.
  2. Fill in the fields. Ones marked with an asterisk are required; the record will not save without them.
  3. Select Save.

Editing a record

  1. Select the row in the list view.
  2. Change what you need to. Save becomes available as soon as something differs.
  3. Select Save.

Deleting a record

  1. Open the record.
  2. Open the actions menu — the ⋯ button beside Save.
  3. Select Delete and confirm.

Two things to know before you start

Changes are live the moment you save

There is no draft stage and no publish step. The moment Save succeeds, the next customer to load the configurator sees the change. There is also no undo. For anything substantial — a new building model, a reworked room layout — it is worth making the change at a quiet hour and checking /configurator straight afterwards.

Deleting can break the records that point at it

Because catalogue records reference one another, removing one leaves a hole in the others. Deleting a region does not delete the product lines sold there, but those lines lose that region from their list. Deleting a room type leaves rooms labelled with a type that no longer exists. Before deleting anything, check what points at it — each chapter says what to look at.

Checking your work

Open /configurator in a second browser tab and reload it after each change. Throughout this guide, panels headed What the customer sees tell you exactly which part of that screen your edit affects.
