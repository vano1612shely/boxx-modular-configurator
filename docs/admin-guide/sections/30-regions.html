Chapter 2Regions

Regions are the markets you sell in. They exist so that one configurator can serve several territories and show each of them only what is actually available there.

Where /admin/collections/regions · Catalog → Regions

What regions are for

A region is a label you attach to the things you sell. Three collections carry a Regions field: Product Lines, Building Models and Furniture Packages. When a customer arrives with a region attached to their visit, they are shown only the records tagged for it.

This is the mechanism behind sentences like "the classroom range is North America only" or "that furniture supplier does not ship to Europe". You do not need regions at all if you sell one catalogue everywhere — leave the collection empty and everything is offered to everyone.

Fields

Region
FieldWhat to put in it
NameRequired The region as a person would say it — United States, Canada, Europe. This is what identifies the region everywhere else in the admin, so make it unambiguous.
CodeRequired A short code used in web addresses and in links from your main website — us, ca, eu. It must be unique across all regions. Capitalisation does not matter when a visitor arrives: US and us reach the same region.

Creating a region

  1. Go to Catalog → Regions.
  2. Select Create New.
  3. Enter the Name, for example United States.
  4. Enter the Code, for example us. Keep it short and lower-case; it will appear in URLs.
  5. Select Save.

A new region starts out attached to nothing. To put it to work, open the product lines, building models or furniture packages you sell there and add it to their Regions field.

Editing a region

Open the region from the list, change what you need, and save. Renaming is safe: everything that points at this region points at the record, not at its text, so a rename updates everywhere at once.

Changing the code breaks existing links

The code appears in the addresses your main website uses to open the configurator. If a page on your site links to /configurator?region=us and you change the code to usa, that link no longer matches a region — and visitors arriving through it will be shown a reduced catalogue, not an error. Change a code only if you can update the links that use it at the same time.

Deleting a region

Open the region, use the ⋯ menu beside Save, and choose Delete.

Deleting a region does not delete anything sold in it. What happens is that every product line, building model and furniture package loses that region from its list. A record left with an empty list is then treated as available everywhere — which is the opposite of what you probably want. Before deleting a region, check what is tagged with it, and give those records the right replacement region first.

What the customer sees

In the configurator

Regions are never shown to the customer. There is no country picker — the region is decided by the address they arrive on, and it silently narrows what they are offered.

A visit is tied to a region by adding region= to the configurator address:

  • /configurator — no region. The customer sees the entire catalogue.
  • /configurator?region=us — the United States catalogue.

Once set, the region follows the customer through the whole session, including when they go back to change their answers. In practice you set this once, on the links from your regional websites, and never think about it again.

The three rules

How a record behaves depends on what is in its Regions field and on what the visitor arrived with.

What gets shown
SituationResult
Record has no regions Shown to everyone, in every region. An empty list means "sold everywhere", not "sold nowhere" — this is what keeps a catalogue that never used regions fully visible.
Record lists the visitor's region Shown.
Record lists other regions only Hidden.
Visitor's code matches no region Only records with no regions at all are shown. A code you cannot identify is treated as a stranger rather than as a guest: showing a region-locked product to an unknown market is exactly what this field exists to prevent.
Building models follow their line

A building model does not usually carry regions of its own — it inherits availability from its product line. If the BOXXPlex line is not sold in Europe, none of its sizes are, even for someone who has a direct link to one. Set regions on models only when a particular size is restricted while the rest of the range is not.

Worked example

You sell two ranges. BOXXPlex offices go everywhere; EDUPlex classrooms are North America only. Set it up like this:

  1. Create two regions: United States / us and Europe / eu.
  2. Open the BOXXPlex product line and leave its Regions field empty — it is sold everywhere.
  3. Open the EDUPlex product line and add United States to its Regions field.
  4. Link your US website to /configurator?region=us and your European site to /configurator?region=eu.

A visitor from the European link is offered BOXXPlex only. A visitor from the US link is offered both. A visitor who reaches /configurator with no region at all is also offered both — so if that is not acceptable, make sure every public link carries a region.
