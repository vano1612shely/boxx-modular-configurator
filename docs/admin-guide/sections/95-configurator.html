Chapter 11Configurator Settings

This is where the configurator's own wording and appearance live — the brand mark it carries, the questions it asks before showing a building, and the units it answers in. Everything a customer chooses between stays in the catalogue; everything they read is here.

Where /admin/globals/configurator-settings · System → Configurator

One record, three tabs

Unlike the collections in this guide, this is a single settings page — there is no list and nothing to create. Open it, change what you need, and select Save. It is organised into three tabs: Page, Quiz and Display.

Emptying a box restores the original wording

Every text box here starts with sensible wording built into the product. Clear one and save, and that original comes back rather than the customer seeing a blank — so there is no way to accidentally ship an empty heading. The two Description boxes under the quiz steps are the deliberate exception: those have no default, and emptying them hides the line entirely.

Page

The marks the configurator carries, and what a link to it looks like in a browser tab, a search result, and a chat window.

Page
FieldWhat it controls
Logo Your brand mark, shown above the quiz questions. Left empty, nothing stands in its place. Whether it appears at all is a switch on the Quiz tab.
Favicon The little icon in the browser tab. A square PNG or SVG. Left empty, the product's own icon is used.
Title The browser tab, the heading in a search result, and the bold line when someone pastes the link into a chat.
Description The grey line under both. Around 160 characters is what a search result shows before it trims.
OG image The picture a shared link unfurls with. 1200 × 630 pixels is what most places crop to. Left empty, the link unfurls without one — deliberately, because a wrong picture is worse than none.

When the catalogue has nothing to show

A Title and Body for the screen a customer reaches when no published building fits their answers. In practice this means a region whose catalogue is empty — so the wording should offer a way to reach you rather than simply apologise.

When the request is larger than any standard size

The screen for a customer who asked for more units than your largest model provides. This is a sales opportunity, not an error: they have told you they want something big.

FieldWhat it controls
ChipThe small badge above the heading.
TitleThe heading.
BodyThe paragraph. Supports two placeholders — see below.
ActionThe button back to the questions, with their answers still in them.
Two placeholders in the body

Write {units} where the number they asked for should appear, and {line} for the product line. For example: "A {units}-office {line} is beyond our standard range — let's talk about a custom build." Anything else in braces is left on screen exactly as typed, so a misspelt placeholder is visible to you rather than silently dropped.

Display

FieldWhat it controls
Area unit Whether floor areas open in square feet or square metres. Square feet by default.

This is the unit the configurator opens in. Customers can switch it for themselves with the small ft²/m² control beside any area figure, and their choice lasts their session without changing this setting. Set it to whichever unit most of your customers think in.

What the customer sees

In the configurator

Everything on this page is customer-facing. It is the only place in the admin where a change is visible without touching the catalogue at all.

After saving, reload /configurator to see quiz and display changes. Page title, description and the shared-link picture are read when the page is requested, so a chat window or search engine may keep showing the old ones for a while — that is caching outside the product, and it settles by itself.
